01 / Networking systems

Deterministic networking & latency modeling

I am interested in how networks can provide predictable service under realistic operating conditions. This includes reasoning about latency bounds, scheduling effects, queueing behavior, and the interaction between deterministic communication mechanisms and heterogeneous systems such as 5G-connected deployments.

Latency modeling is central here: the goal is not only to measure delays, but to explain where they come from and how they compose across an end-to-end path.

02 / Timing systems

Clock synchronization

Clock synchronization remains a core theme in my work. I study how synchronization uncertainty accumulates across systems and network paths, with attention to timestamping, queueing, switch behavior, oscillator stability, and model-based interpretation of timing measurements.

A recurring objective is to make timing quality measurable and explainable, especially in settings where systems need to make decisions based on imperfect time information.

03 / Models & evaluation

Digital twins

I am interested in digital twins as a way to represent complex technical systems closely enough to support analysis, experimentation, and operational reasoning. In networking and distributed systems, that means combining measurements, models, and assumptions into artifacts that can be interrogated and validated.

This direction connects naturally to system evaluation, what-if analysis, and the translation between observed behavior and the abstractions used to reason about it.

04 / Algorithms

Tree splitting algorithms

Tree splitting algorithms are interesting to me as a bridge between theory and systems behavior. They offer a structured way to resolve contention and study randomized coordination, especially when many participants compete for shared resources or access opportunities.

This line of work fits naturally with broader interests in performance modeling, distributed coordination, and scalable algorithm design.
