Research

Making network timing measurable and predictable

My research interests sit at the intersection of computer networking, distributed systems, and precision time synchronization.

01 / Timing systems

Clock synchronization and uncertainty

Precision Time Protocol (PTP) systems depend on packet-delay behavior, timestamping, and oscillator stability. I study how uncertainty accumulates across network paths and how measurement-driven models can help characterize the quality of synchronization.

Topics include per-hop delay distributions, switch processing latency, queueing effects, hardware timestamping, and temperature-dependent oscillator drift.

02 / Data planes

Programmable networks for timing

Programmable switches provide opportunities to observe and influence packet handling close to the wire. I investigate how data-plane mechanisms can support timing traffic and how congestion in different queues affects time-sensitive packets.

03 / Systems

Datacenter networking and measurement

I use simulation, experiments, and hardware measurements to understand network behavior under realistic traffic conditions. A recurring goal is to connect system-level guarantees with observable hardware behavior.

Methods

How I approach research

Model

Develop analytical and stochastic models for network delay and clock uncertainty.

Measure

Validate assumptions with packet traces, hardware timestamping, and switch/NIC experiments.

Evaluate

Use simulation and real systems to understand accuracy, scalability, and trade-offs.
